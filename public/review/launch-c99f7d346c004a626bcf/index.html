THE BRAND & PLAN

THE STORY

A favor becomes an open road

The trip begins by helping a friend move from New York to Vancouver. After Vancouver, the plan becomes a solo journey: remote work, national parks, the coast, and the long way home.

The audience should recognize Sonny before it recognizes a travel channel: candid reactions, dry jokes, imperfect plans, real people, and wonder without pretending every day was effortless.

Positioning: a personal road diary with cinematic scenery. It is not a list of travel tips, a promise to teach risky activities, or a guide to every destination.

Three acts: the convoy west (E01–08); the solo coast (E09–25); the inland return and home (E26–36). Keep the original motive clear before describing freedom or spontaneity.

IDENTITY

One route. One visual language.

Keep the approved route-line logo. Its geometry comes from the actual journey and includes the Southern California correction. Do not redraw it as a generic U.S. outline or use an uncorrected route.

Primary display type: Bebas Neue Regular, supplied with its OFL. Utility copy: Arial or a platform-native sans serif. Detailed pixel illustrations belong to story graphics; they do not replace the display identity.

Core palette: ink #101B23, ivory #F5F1E7, amber #E6B56A. Supporting muted text: #A8B6BC. Use ivory on ink; use amber as an accent rather than on every word.

Leave breathing room around the logo: at least the height of the 2025 line. Scale proportionally. Route-only marks are supplied in amber, ivory, and ink; avatars add a readable title because a thin route disappears at small sizes.

VISUAL SYSTEM

The place first, the graphic second

Use one real, recognizable moment in each cover. Keep the main subject clear. The pack uses original photographs, clean prepared pictures, and accepted v6 frames, avoiding the corrupt v7 composites.

Covers use a short headline, a place name, an episode tag, and restrained ink. Landscape images can use a gradient; portrait photographs retain their whole shape on an ink panel. No fake scenery, invented reactions, or before/after claims.

Magnets remain real photographic cutouts. Use one relevant magnet for a location marker, or an accumulating collection at a genuine payoff. Do not imply every magnet represents a national park.

Vertical covers preserve the whole source image above the title rather than stretching or forcing landscape scenery into a tall crop. These are cover graphics, not previews of the entire vertical edit. Check each cover in the target app before posting.

CHANNEL ASSETS

Make the account feel like the film

YouTube banner: 2560 × 1440 PNG. All live copy sits inside a conservative central 1544 × 422 area. A separate guide image shows that area and is clearly marked NOT FOR UPLOAD.

Avatar: 1024 × 1024 PNG, dark and ivory. Watermark: transparent 512 × 512 PNG. Story cards: 1080 × 1920. End card: 3840 × 2160 with space for platform end-screen elements.

YouTube covers: 3840 × 2160 JPEG plus 1280 × 720 mobile-friendly variants. Vertical covers: 1080 × 1920 JPEG. Editable SVG cover files and their reference photos are included.

The 57-second approved movie opening is not replaced by the older three-second brand-kit motion file. This pack includes the approved animated logo SVG as an identity asset, not a new film intro.

VOICE & COPY

Sound like Sonny

First person. Concrete place names. Honest stakes: missing the ferry, working by the beach, finding the view, getting home. Keep titles inviting without manufacturing danger or a life-changing lesson.

The descriptions lead with the episode, then connect it to the whole trip. Do not put a production-method explanation ahead of the travel story. Codex and Claude are credited briefly after the story.

Public social hooks can be cleaner than the full film without changing Sonny’s personality. Do not use private conversations, salary details, explicit discussion of another private person, or nudity as bait.

Canonical figures: 11,701 miles on the odometer; 11,606 on the mapped route. In-transit totals stay in their original context. The final figures must not be presented as competing estimates.

YOUTUBE

The episodes are the destination

Publish the flat landscape episode as the main series entry. Keep the 360 edition in a clearly labeled companion playlist. The chapter supercuts and full film are alternative ways to watch, not duplicate numbered episodes.

For each episode, choose the primary title or test its alternative later. Use the supplied description and pinned comment, then add real playlist and next-episode URLs once those uploads exist.

Verify each final delivery receipt before upload. E01, E08, E18, and E31 are in the repair queue as this pack is built. Do not upload by choosing whichever file has the newest modification date.

Chapters are deliberately not supplied with invented timings. Generate them from the final uploaded edition after repairs and any later additions. Use automatic captions as a draft and proof place names and clipped speech.

TIKTOK & SHORTS

One moment, one reason to keep watching

Three ideas per episode are supplied, 108 total. They are editorial leads, not finished clips. Choose a moment with a clear reveal, a short story, or a reaction followed by what caused it.

Start on an image or sentence that makes sense without the full episode. Keep natural dialogue intact, add small breath buffers, and end on a payoff. Do not chop a sentence just to fit a round runtime.

Draft target: often 15–45 seconds. The right length depends on the moment; E07 is only about 33 seconds in the full cut, so its three ideas are alternatives, not three must-post duplicates.

Suggested working safe area on a 1080 × 1920 canvas: essential text inside x90–900 and y240–1450. This is a conservative design guide, not an official permanent UI boundary. Check the preview in each app.

LAUNCH PLAN

Steady releases, not a dump of 36 files

Suggested cadence: two flat episodes per week over 18 weeks. Pair each with a 360 companion if its final upload is ready. Use two or three distinct short moments between episodes; publish only the best ideas.

Before launch: verify the repaired master and episodes; choose covers; create the two playlists; set the channel banner and bio; prepare a trailer; schedule a small first batch instead of the whole season.

Launch sequence: introduce the real trip motive, release E01, post a stand-alone Chicago moment, release E02, then preview the first park. The planner gives relative weeks; no launch date is assumed.

Use your own audience data to set posting times. Review title/cover clarity, early retention, average percentage viewed, and whether shorts lead viewers to an episode. Avoid treating raw views across platforms as the same metric.

AUDIO & RIGHTS

Make the story audible and the release reviewable

Speech leads. Music sits underneath it. Use location sound where it adds atmosphere, and keep complete dialogue. Match the first and second halves of the film rather than normalizing isolated clips without listening.

The Red Rocks gag is an editorial choice, not proof of copyright clearance. Before public upload, confirm the actual concert audio remaining in the final file, the treatment of visible performance footage, and any platform claims.

Keep a track ledger for Suno and locally generated music: file, episode, generator, generation date, account/terms evidence, and any samples or references. AI generation alone does not establish a platform-ready license.

Shorts longer than one minute with an active Content ID claim can be blocked globally on YouTube. TikTok’s AI-labeling rules require a separate check against the audio and visuals actually used. A visual blur does not settle audio rights.

360 DELIVERY

Invite people to look around

Use the separate immersive exports, not a flat reframing placed inside a sphere. Preserve projection metadata and test that the YouTube player exposes pan/look-around controls before publishing.

Describe these as monoscopic 360° videos with stereo audio. Do not label them stereoscopic 3D, VR180, or spatial audio unless a future export actually supplies those features.

Desktop, phone, and headset are all part of review. The website’s xrgallery player and YouTube playback are separate targets, so a successful test on one does not prove the other.

Keep baked-in graphics as approved. This launch pack does not bring back the rejected floating headset HUD or alter the current immersive edit.
